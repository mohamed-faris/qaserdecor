Gypsum wall decoration and TV feature walls

A wall detail succeeds when it organises the elevation and hides the practical work without becoming visually noisy.

Published 6 October 20266 minute read
Editorial note

This is general planning guidance. The final specification depends on site measurement, project requirements and the approved system.

Measure the whole elevation

Do not design only the area behind the television. Record the full wall, adjacent doors, curtains, skirting and sight line from the main seating. The composition needs to resolve at both ends.

Mark a centre line and the finished television size. Many wall designs fail because the framing was based on a smaller screen or because the screen was centred independently from the room.

Plan support before gypsum board

Televisions, floating cabinets and shelves need specified backing and appropriate anchors. The weight should transfer to suitable support, not to the face board alone.

If a future screen size is uncertain, provide a practical support zone rather than one tiny fixing point. Photograph the backing and record dimensions before closing the wall.

Keep cables useful and accessible

Decide where power, data, receiver boxes and sound equipment will sit. A conduit is useful only when its bends and diameter allow the intended connectors to pass through.

Avoid sealing adapters or power supplies inside an inaccessible cavity. Provide ventilation and access where equipment creates heat.

Use depth with restraint

Shallow layers often create enough shadow. Deep boxes consume floor area and can make a narrow room feel tighter. Repeated niches also need a real purpose; otherwise they collect clutter.

For a classic wall, align moulded frames with nearby openings. For a modern wall, keep the main planes broad and let one reveal or light line do the work.

Review at eye level

Ceiling plans are usually reviewed from below, but wall details are experienced straight on and from close range. Check socket heights, joint lines and corner alignment while standing where the furniture will be.

Before paint, inspect the wall with side lighting. It will expose uneven jointing and waviness that a front-facing work light can hide.
