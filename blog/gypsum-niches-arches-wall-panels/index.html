Designing gypsum niches, arches and wall panels

Curves and recesses feel crafted when their dimensions follow the wall; they feel decorative when they are merely added to it.

Published 6 October 20266 minute read
Editorial note

This is general planning guidance. The final specification depends on site measurement, project requirements and the approved system.

Give every recess a reason

A niche can display an object, hold indirect light or break up a long wall. Decide its use before setting the size. An opening designed around nothing often becomes awkward storage.

Check the viewing angle and the height of furniture below it. In a passage, keep projections and sharp corners away from movement.

Draw arches at full scale

A small change in radius can make an arch feel formal, soft or pointed. Label the spring line, width and height rather than asking site labour to interpret a photograph.

For repeated arches, use one template and verify the first opening before building the rest. Consistency is more visible than decorative complexity.

Control the depth of wall panels

Layered panels need only enough depth to create a clean shadow. Excessive build-up reduces usable area and complicates sockets, skirting and door frames.

Align panel margins with nearby architecture. Narrow leftover strips at corners are a sign that the layout needs to be adjusted.

Integrate light without exposing it

If a niche includes light, establish the profile, cable route, driver access and the surface that will receive the glow. The source should not be visible from the normal eye line.

Test one detail before repeating it. Warm light can enrich relief, but it also highlights uneven corners and jointing.

Use samples for repeated details

Mark one panel or curve on the wall and approve its scale. For a complex moulded centre, review the actual profile depth and spacing under site light.

Once approved, record the dimensions and repeat them. Craft comes from controlled repetition, not from changing each element as the work moves across the room.
